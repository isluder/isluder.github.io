Isaac Sluder

Ph.D. Candidate | Mechanical Engineering Researcher

Bridging the gap between advanced composite materials and data-driven systems engineering.

I am a third-year Ph.D. candidate and Graduate Research Assistant at the University of Akron's Advanced Metacomposites Laboratory, advised by Dr. Kwek-Tze (K.T.) Tan. I completed Ph.D. candidacy in May 2026 and expect to graduate in summer 2028. I am also pursuing a certificate in Computational Data Science. My work combines materials informatics, polymer and composite environmental durability, and machine learning.

My foundational research investigated the hygrothermal degradation of 3D-printed composites (such as PA6 and PAHT-CF) through extensive environmental testing, resulting in a first-author publication in Composites Part B: Engineering. My current research focuses on automated extraction of polymer degradation data via large language models. I am developing Matract, a testbed for evaluating extraction pipelines, and a pipeline for curating hygrothermal degradation data from polyimide and bismaleimide literature. I use Python, Neo4j/Cypher, and SQLite to turn scientific literature into structured, traceable datasets.

From May to August 2026, I completed a summer research internship in the Polymer Matrix Composites Branch at the Air Force Research Laboratory (AFRL), Wright-Patterson AFB. I developed an end-to-end large language model pipeline for screening, extracting, normalizing, and organizing a 1,452-document corpus on polyimide and bismaleimide hygrothermal degradation into a provenance-tracked Neo4j knowledge graph. The pipeline also supported literature-grounded interactive reports with plots and chemical structure diagrams. For a 1,500-paper corpus, the workflow reduced an estimated 18 full-time chemist-weeks of processing to 20 hours and approximately $140 in compute and API costs. Long-term, my goal is to design intelligent systems that improve engineering decision-making through advanced databases, machine learning, and LLM pipelines.

When I step away from the lab or the terminal, you can usually find me training for my next run, leading workouts and rucks with F3 RubberCity, dialing in a home espresso, reading, or playing guitar. Feel free to explore my site to see my latest research and publications.
